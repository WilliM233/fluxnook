## 1. Feasibility (Nookkeeper)

- **The notes, measured** (Python `csv` over the committed CSV, 2026-10-23). 60 films have a note; two notes have two lines (positions 8, 28), so 62 note lines; one is empty (52). Every line matches `<Form>: <Title>[, <Collection>] (<Year>)` with Form one of `Novel` (19 lines), `Short Story` (38), `Novella` (1), `Series` (1), `Bachman Novel` (1). Distinct works after dedupe on (form, title, collection, year): 7 novels, 1 novella, 1 series, 1 Bachman novel, 16 stories, all 16 in `Night Shift (1977)`. Plus Night Shift itself, which no line names as a work but 16 lines name as the container: 27 items. Children of the Corn is one story with 12 films.
- **Two parsing traps.** (1) `Short Story: Quitters, Inc, Night Shift (1977)`: the title has a comma. The collection is what follows the *last* `, ` before ` (`, and only when Form is `Short Story`; a greedy-left split yields title "Quitters" and collection "Inc, Night Shift". (2) Multi-line notes end their first line with `\r` on a CRLF read; Ch 0.4's `csv.mjs` already normalizes `\r\n`, so the builder sees `\n` (confirmed by `king-films.json`'s `source_note` for Chapelwaite). The builder must split note lines on `\n` after that normalization, never on `\r`.
- **Form and author mapping.** `Novel` → form novel, author Stephen King. `Bachman Novel` → form novel, author Richard Bachman. `Novella`, `Series` → forms novella, series, author Stephen King. `Short Story` → form story, author Stephen King, plus the collection. Night Shift → form collection, author Stephen King, year from the lines (1977, all agree; the builder checks they agree and stops if not).
- **Dedupe and ids.** Key is (form, title, collection, year). Slug: lowercase, every run of non-alphanumerics to one hyphen, trimmed (`Salem's Lot` → `salems-lot`, `Quitters, Inc` → `quitters-inc`, `I am the Doorway` → `i-am-the-doorway`). No two works share a slug and year (checked). `manual:` satisfies the shape's id regex and has no `providerUrl` entry, so the S4 refinement (an id's provider must match its URL) doesn't apply; `providers: {}` is the "none on a non-provider id" case Ch 0.4's test 8 already guards. The shape's `providers` enum stays `["letterboxd"]`; Open Library is added when it exists.
- **`source_note` on a work** is the first note line that produced it (position order). It's the provenance for Volume 1 Ch 3's reconciliation; the film's own note stays on the film.
- **Relations across files.** `king-films.json` has the film ids; `king-books.json` has the works and all 78 relations. The shape's `superRefine` is per file; A1's cross-file rule lives in the test (`tests/unit/fixtures.test.ts`), which loads every `*.json` under `supabase/seed/fixtures/`, unions the ids, and checks uniqueness and resolution. A relation type's direction is fixed by its name: `adaptation-of` from the adaptation to the source, `contained-in` from the part to the whole; the test can also assert the endpoint types (adaptation-of: film → book; contained-in: book → book with form collection) as a Rummage candidate.
- **The shelf order.** Year, then the position of the first film whose *first* note line names the work. That puts Night Shift (first primary at position 12, Graveyard Shift) after The Shining (10) in 1977, Chapelwaite's second line notwithstanding; Dead Zone (53) before Firestarter (55) in 1979; Cujo (59), The Dark Tower (60), The Running Man (61) in 1981. Deterministic from the file; the description names the rule.
- **Two builders, one raw file.** `scripts/fixtures-build.mjs`'s `SOURCES` gets a second line with the same `raw` and a different `build` and `out`. The films builder is untouched; A2's byte-for-byte check on `king-films.json` is the proof, and the existing fresh-build test covers both outputs once the second is in `SOURCES`.
- **`gaps`.** New optional top-level array in the shape, `{ item: itemId, reason: string }`, items resolving like any id. One entry today. The loader decides what to do with it; this chapter only records it.
- **Biome and the JSON.** `serializeFixture` already matches Biome's formatter (Ch 0.4 Deferral); a second file changes nothing. The relations array will be the largest thing in the file (78 entries), still well under anything that matters.
- **Risk, low.** The regex for the note line is the only new parser; it fails loudly on a line it can't read (stop the build naming record and line), which is the right behavior for a hand-written note, and today every line parses.
